01

Monthly trends

Average daily supply dipped hardest in March (11.1h/day, 4.1h average shortfall) and recovered through the middle of the year, climbing back to 15.1h/day by August — the best month in the dataset.

02

Weekly rhythm

Supply is remarkably flat across the week — under half an hour of variation between the best day (Sunday, 13.9h) and the softest (Friday, 13.1h). Unlike consumer demand, grid allocation here doesn't follow a strong weekday/weekend pattern.

03

Reliability, by the hour — not the band

Every feeder is assigned a "band" — a regulatory cap on daily hours (4, 8, 12, 16, or 24h). Grading reliability against that cap alone is misleading: a feeder hitting 100% of an 8-hour band is still only delivering 8 hours a day. So reliability here is graded on absolute hours out of 24, regardless of band.

05

The forecasting model

A one-day-ahead demand model (gradient-boosted trees), trained on Jan–Jun, validated on July, tested on August — a genuine time-based split, no leakage. 7-day forecasts are produced recursively, feeding each day's prediction back in as the next day's lag features.

What the model actually relies on

Yesterday's actual hours (LAG_1D) dominates — expected for a highly autocorrelated series like grid supply. Weather is currently synthetic (no live feed connected yet), so its low importance here isn't yet meaningful.
